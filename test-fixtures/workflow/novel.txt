Chapter One — A Quiet Village

The village of Amberwood sat at the edge of a forgotten forest, where the oaks grew taller than cathedrals and the wind carried rumours from distant lands. Alice had lived there all her life, helping her father at the bakery, waking before dawn to knead dough and stoke the stone oven. She knew every cobblestone in the square, every creak in the warped floorboards of the inn, every drunkard who slumped against the fountain after midnight.

But tonight, something was different. A stranger had come into the village with the last coach from the north, his coat dusted with ash, his eyes too bright for the lamplight. He paid for his room in silver coins and took his supper alone. The innkeeper whispered to her husband in the kitchen, and even the bakery cat stopped its rounds to stare through the front window.

Alice set down her rolling pin. She wiped flour from her hands and walked to the door. Outside, the bell of St Elias's chapel tolled the hour. Nine strokes. The moon hung low over the pines, silvering the edge of the forest road.

Chapter Two — An Offer

The stranger's name was Corvin. He said he was a cartographer, hired by the duke to remap the old trade routes. The innkeeper, who trusted nobody, trusted him enough to rent his best room. The schoolmaster, who trusted everyone, shook his hand like an old friend. But Alice was not convinced.

She watched Corvin over breakfast the next morning. He ate slowly, and he watched the village watch him. When he was done he rose, paid his bill, and walked directly to the bakery. Alice was cleaning the ovens. She froze when the bell above the door chimed.

"I heard you bake the best rye in three counties," he said. His voice was soft, almost musical. "I would like to order two loaves. And I would like to ask you a favour."

Alice wiped her hands on her apron. "A favour?"

"I need a guide. Someone who knows the forest paths. Someone who keeps their mouth shut when asked to."

She stared at him. He met her stare without flinching.

"Why me?"

"Because," he said, "you are the only person in this village who has looked me in the eye since I arrived."

Alice thought about this for a long moment. Then she turned back to the oven. "Two loaves," she said. "I'll have them ready at noon. We can talk about your favour then."

Chapter Three — The Forest Road

At noon she handed him the loaves, still warm. At half past noon she had packed a small canvas bag with cheese, dried apples, and a sharp knife. At one o'clock they were walking the forest road together, and by the time the sun dipped behind the trees Alice realised she had not asked Corvin where they were going.

"Amberwood," he said, answering the question she had not asked. "The old one. Before the fire."

"There is no old Amberwood."

"There was. And there still is, if you know where the road forks."

The forest was quiet, the kind of quiet that comes after a sudden wind. Somewhere a raven called. Alice adjusted the strap of her bag and stepped over a fallen branch. Whatever Corvin was looking for, she had a feeling she was going to find it first.

Chapter Four — The Fork

They reached the fork at dusk. The road split into three paths: one straight, one bending east, one vanishing under a curtain of moss into a hollow of stones. Corvin stopped. He looked at Alice. "Which one?"

"You tell me."

He smiled. It was not a pleasant smile.

"I was hoping," he said, "you would tell me."
